    Research agenda

    Workplace Behavioural Science in Taiwan

    Starting from real situations in Taiwanese workplaces, this strand asks how working conditions shape the way people spot, report and deal with hazards.

    Open question These are open questions, a starting point for future case observation and site trials.

    Time pressure and the decision to stop work
    When the schedule is tight or handover information is thin, what shapes a worker's decision to pause the job?
    Supervisor response and hazard reporting
    How does a supervisor's first response, and whether they report back on progress, affect an employee's willingness to report next time?
    Work design and control measures
    How do forms, tools and equipment affect whether safety steps get done? In a redesign trial, which steps are most often stuck or skipped?

    About

    HSE from the Field

    Senior HSE Manager | Writer & Podcaster | Knowledge Distiller

    Every topic is chosen with one test in mind: can a notice, an investigation or an improvement case become a check or practice the site can use next week?

    Each weekly briefing covers recent developments in Taiwan, the US, the UK, Japan and Europe, keeping event dates, notice dates and data cut-off dates separate. The essays are the author's own views.

    Everything here is free to read. If it helps you, small contributions are welcome.

    How sources are labelled

    • Official source

      Regulator notices, incident investigations and case reports published by institutions. Each links to the original, and the original's figures prevail.

    • Author's analysis

      The author's reading and field practices based on hands-on experience; not the position of the original institution.

    • Open question

      Research questions without answers yet, and events still under investigation or with unknown causes.
