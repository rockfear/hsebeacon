HSE WEEKLY / VOL. 04

After the expansion, does management still work? Outside consultation, stress follow-ups and collaborative robots

This issue asks whether management measures keep working after work expands and technology changes. This week's developments cover dust monitoring, work-related ill health statistics and an offshore structures audit. The three in-depth cases, a US metal processor, a UK scientific instrument maker and an Italian electronics manufacturer, were not covered in depth in the previous three issues. Verifiable company improvements from the last seven days were limited, so the dates of the older cases and why they were chosen are set out below.

01 / REGIONAL SCAN

Developments in five regions

Each item shows this week's development and its evidence status separately.Official source

Taiwan

On 20 Nov the OSHA Taiwan website listed two notices, effective 1 Jan 2027: added crystalline free silica concentration monitoring for designated industries and specific dust workplaces, and rules on who is monitored and on sampling numbers and frequency. On 18 Nov it also reported an exchange with Korea's KGS on hydrogen safety systems and technology.

Evidence statusThese are regulatory and exchange developments; whether company exposure improves remains to be monitored. Check the notice annexes for the scope and details on dust. This issue does not reuse the engineered stone case from Issue 01 as an in-depth case.

United States

On 12 Nov the CSB released a safety video on remote isolation, using three 2019 cases, ITC, PES and TPC, to show that manual or local valves can be impossible to reach safely during a fire, so material keeps feeding it.

Evidence statusThis falls outside the seven-day window and is listed as process safety background. The video draws on a 2024 study and gives no new incident findings this week or evidence that recommendations have been completed.

CSB remote isolation safety video ↗

United Kingdom

The 2025/26 annual work-related ill health and injury statistics were released on 19 Nov; the HSE press release is dated 20 Nov. The data cover April 2025 to March 2026. An estimated 891,000 workers suffered work-related stress, depression or anxiety, with 18.2 million working days lost.

Evidence statusHSE notes that overall rates of work-related ill health and related days lost have been broadly flat over the last four years, so a fall in the annual figures needs reading against the long-term trend.

Japan

On 20 Nov the Tokyo Labour Bureau updated its construction safety and health measures, setting accident prevention requirements in response to a rise in deaths.

Evidence statusThis is a regulator action notice; there is no evidence yet of fewer accidents after the intervention.

Tokyo Labour Bureau updates ↗

Europe (excluding UK)

On 20 Nov Norway's Havtil published an audit of ConocoPhillips' in-service monitoring of load-bearing structures, carried out on site on 19–20 Oct. It identified one nonconformity, concerning the design loads and collision resistance of bridges that visiting vessels could strike, and asked the operator to reply by 11 Dec on how it will be dealt with.

Evidence statusThis is an identified nonconformity; repair and verification await further information.

02 / Field notes

Field notes for offshore wind and contractor management

Author's analysis

This week's structures audit and the earlier remote isolation study suggest two questions for the weekly meeting: after vessel specifications or operating practices change, have collision design and berthing conditions been reviewed again? In an incident, can operators still reach isolation points safely? This is a cross-industry extension; specific controls should be assessed against site design, equipment data and emergency scenarios.

Risk management and improvement cases from three regions

None of the three was covered in depth in the previous three issues. Verifiable company improvements this week were limited, so each case notes the official page date and why it was chosen.

01 / United States2025SHARP renewed (no recordable injuries or illnesses in 2024)
Official page gives no publication date · Outside consultation

RMP metal processing: sustaining the safety system with outside consultation

Background and main risks.Rolled Metal Products (RMP) in Tennessee has 28 employees and does precision processing of metal coils, including slitting and cutting. Given the work, the site needs to assess risks such as caught-in hazards from coils, sharp edges and material handling. That is this briefing's analysis; the official case does not list specific accidents.

Dates and confirmed results.Official source The company has worked with TOSHA on-site consultation since 2013, first earned SHARP in 2018, was renewed in 2020, had no recordable injuries or illnesses in 2024 and was renewed again in 2025. The official page gives no publication date, and no new results were found this week. The case was chosen to show how a small traditional processor keeps its system going over the long term.

Professional management, challenges and solutions.The official case says the company used outside consultation to revise its programme, fix deficiencies and strengthen its management system. For small plants with limited staff, this briefing suggests using outside expertise to fill gaps in assessment, while site supervisors keep responsibility for doing and verifying the work. The public material lists no specific engineering changes, no results per measure and no full exposure hours, so no single measure's contribution can be quantified.

What anyone can borrow Author's analysis

Pick one recurring deficiency, record an owner, a completion date and on-site verification, and have a colleague or expert who knows the task review it regularly. In a year with zero recordable injuries, also check that high-risk controls were actually carried out.

02 / United Kingdom2employees off sick with work stress, both back at work
Official page updated 2021.05.14 · Stress management

Singer Instruments: turning stress signals into role and staffing changes

Background and management challenge.Official source The scientific instrument company in this HSE case has 45 employees. At first, two employees went off sick with work-related stress. The company kept in touch, arranged phased returns and adjusted their roles, and both returned to work. A Management Standards survey then identified a lack of manager support and of ways to share concerns.

Specific measures and confirmed results.The quality and safety manager offered a communication channel across departments. After the company expanded, demand indicators for the assembly department changed; the safety manager used confidential interviews to understand workload concerns, then added staff and introduced cross-training. At a follow-up a few weeks later, the employees who had raised concerns said they had been addressed. These are return-to-work outcomes and interview feedback; the source does not quantify any fall in incident rates or sick leave.

Dates and why this case.The official page was updated on 14 May 2021 and describes the case and follow-ups over the years without exact dates for each intervention. This issue uses it to respond to the burden of work-related ill health highlighted by the new statistics on 19 Nov, and to rotate towards technical services and organisational behaviour. It is an older case; no new improvements were confirmed this week.

What anyone can borrow, and the Taiwan context Author's analysis

When a team says the workload is too heavy, first use confidential interviews to find which handover, process step or skill is the bottleneck, then follow up after adjusting staffing and work arrangements. Bringing this into Taiwanese workplaces, one could study whether supervisor responses and real adjustments make people more willing to raise risks again; tracking indicators need defining first, with appropriate consent.

03 / Italy~60,000manual handling operations removed per year, 5–30 kg each
Published 2025.05.14 · Automation and ergonomics

Dinamica Generale: cutting repetitive handling through automation

Background and main risks.Official source The company supplies electronic equipment and sensors for precision agriculture, medical and other fields. As it grew, it faced ergonomic risks in feeding parts, lifting heavy items and at workstations. The EU-OSHA case was published on 14 May 2025 and does not give the start date of each of the three systems. It was chosen to show how an emerging sensor industry reduces exposure in its own processes; no new results were found this week.

Specific measures.A collaborative robot with a 30 kg payload was introduced to move workpieces for strain gauge bonding equipment, with process parameters set by barcode. An automatic cleaning system and electronically controlled handling aids also reduce manual handling and contact with chemical cleaning.

Confirmed operational results and limits.EU-OSHA records that the robot workstation removed manual handling of about 60,000 workpieces a year, each weighing 5–30 kg. The three systems cost about €344,000 in total. The figure shows manual handling demand removed; there are no full before-and-after accident rates to compare, and no residual risk assessment for full maintenance interventions has been published.

Challenges and practices to borrow Author's analysis

Only after the robot workstation succeeded did the company extend investment to cleaning and handling aids. For Taiwanese companies, this briefing suggests starting with one frequent task with a clear load, verifying handling counts, postures and how abnormal situations are handled, before deciding to scale up. Collaborative robots still need an on-site risk assessment for pinch points, approach and maintenance work.

04 / This issue's essay

Everyone says it's fine. How does a manager know it really is?

Why this pick.The Singer Instruments case gives work stress management a concrete setting: first two people off sick, then business expansion after their return, with the team needing to keep checking that support is working. It links well to HSE Beacon's workplace behavioural science strand in Taiwan.

Angle.Open with a meeting scene clearly marked as hypothetical: the manager asks whether the work can be finished on time, there is silence, then everyone nods. Then bring in the case's confidential interviews, staffing changes and follow-up a few weeks later, and ask how long it took from first raising a difficulty to getting it dealt with. Close by inviting listeners to pick one work difficulty raised recently and confirm who is handling it, when they will reply, and how they will know the change worked. For research, response times and willingness to report could be compared before and after changes, leaving room to verify causality.
